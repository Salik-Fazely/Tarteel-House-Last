A CALM FIRST FOUNDATION

Building clear letter sounds before rushing to full pages

For a child who has never read Arabic before, the first months set habits that stay for years. In a crowded class, a child who mixes up two similar letters often keeps guessing rather than stopping to ask. In a live one-to-one session over Zoom or Google Meet, the teacher hears every sound clearly and slows down whenever a letter needs extra care.

Whether your family calls the beginner primer Noorani Qaida, Qaida, or Elif-Ba, the purpose is the same: helping your child recognise Arabic letters in every shape, connect vowel marks calmly, and move steadily into reading short verses from the Mushaf with confidence.

Every lesson is 40 minutes long, and your child normally continues with the same dedicated teacher. Every two months, we invite parents to a 15-minute progress review. If a parent is unavailable or does not want a meeting, we send a visual progress report through WhatsApp instead.

STEP BY STEP

What your child covers in beginner reading lessons

There is no fixed timetable pushed onto every child. Your teacher begins from what your child already knows and moves forward at a pace that keeps lessons calm.

1. Individual Arabic letters

Recognising letters clearly by shape and sound, including letters that sound similar to children growing up in Europe or North America.

2. Connected forms and vowels

Reading joined letters with short vowels (fatha, kasra, damma), tanween, sukoon, and shaddah without guessing from memory.

3. Early rules of recitation

Learning natural lengthening (madd) and clear stops gently through reading practice, preparing smoothly for Tajweed lessons.

4. First verses from the Mushaf

Transitioning from primer lines to short surahs in Juz Amma, ending each 40-minute lesson with a simple home practice focus.

QUESTIONS PARENTS ASK

Before you begin

My five-year-old knows zero Arabic letters — is that fine?

Yes. Many students begin as complete beginners between ages 5 and 8, and older beginners aged 9 to 16 are equally welcome. See when to start learning Qur’an.

Are classes live with a real teacher?

Yes. Every lesson is a live one-to-one session with a human teacher over Zoom or Google Meet, never an automated app or pre-recorded video. Lessons are not recorded by Tarteel House by default.

Can lessons be taught in Persian or Turkish?

Yes. Explanations can be in English, Arabic, Turkish, or Persian while the Qur’an is always read in Arabic. See Farsi & Dari classes or Turkish classes.

What comes after Noorani Qaida?

Once a child reads joined words comfortably, their same teacher guides them naturally into fluent recitation, Tajweed, and short-surah memorisation.
