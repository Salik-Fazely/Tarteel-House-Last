RAMADAN

A Ramadan Quran Plan for Kids: Simple Daily Goals by Age

Plan a calm Ramadan Quran routine for your child with realistic daily goals for ages 5 to 16, a four-week structure, revision ideas and tips for school days.

Ramadan is the month in which the Quran was revealed, and many families want it to be a time when their children grow closer to the Quran too.

It is also easy to aim too high. A child who starts Ramadan with a very ambitious goal can feel discouraged by the second week. A small, steady routine that lasts all month is usually worth more than an intense first few days.

In 2027, Ramadan is expected to begin around 7–8 February, depending on the sighting of the moon and your local mosque’s announcement. That leaves time to plan calmly and to start a light routine before the month begins.

Choose one main goal for the month

One clear goal is easier for a child to understand than several goals at once. Choose the one that fits your child’s current level:

  • Daily reading: read or practise every day, even briefly.
  • Memorisation: learn one or more short surahs, or secure surahs already started.
  • Revision: strengthen surahs your child has memorised but no longer recites confidently.
  • Pronunciation: improve one specific point the teacher has mentioned.

If your child already has a Quran teacher, ask them which goal is realistic. The teacher knows what your child can currently manage.

Daily goals by age

These are starting points, not rules. Adjust them to your child’s reading level, energy and school timetable.

Suggested daily Ramadan goals

Should my child try to finish the whole Quran?

Many adults aim to complete a full reading of the Quran (a khatm) during Ramadan, often by reading about one juz each day. For most children this is not realistic, and trying can turn reading into a race.

A gentler idea is a family khatm: each family member takes some of the thirty parts, and the household completes the Quran together. A child might take a single juz, a few pages or a set of short surahs from Juz Amma. They still share in the family’s goal without carrying an adult’s target.

A simple four-week structure

Ramadan in four weeks

Week Main focus
Week 1Settle the routine. Keep sessions short and find the time of day that works.
Week 2Build steadily. Add new material only if the routine is holding.
Week 3Consolidate. Spend more time on revision than on new verses.
Last ten nightsA little extra effort, a calm family recitation in the evening, and time to talk about the meaning of Surah Al-Qadr.

Around Eid, look back together at what your child achieved. Name specific progress: “You recited Al-Kawthar every evening this month.”

Fitting Quran practice around school days

For families in Europe and North America, Ramadan 2027 falls during the school term. In February, the days are relatively short in much of Europe, so iftar comes early in the evening. That can leave a calm window for family Quran time after the meal.

  • Link practice to something that already happens, such as straight after iftar or just before bed.
  • Keep weekday sessions short and use weekends for a little more revision.
  • Avoid the most tired moments, such as the time just before iftar if your child is practising fasting.
  • Plan for busy days. On a difficult day, two minutes of revision still keeps the routine alive.

Children are not required to fast before they reach puberty, but many families introduce fasting gradually. A child who is practising fasting may be more tired than usual, so keep Quran sessions short and well timed.

Keep motivation gentle

  • Use a simple Ramadan chart where your child marks each day of practice.
  • Let your child help choose which short surah to learn.
  • Praise effort and consistency, not only finished surahs.
  • Avoid comparing siblings or cousins.
  • Let your child hear you recite too. Children notice what the adults around them value.

If practice has become a struggle before Ramadan even starts, our guide on what to do when a child refuses Quran practice may help.

Keep a smaller routine after Ramadan

Many children practise well during Ramadan and then stop completely after Eid. Before the month ends, agree on a smaller routine to continue, for example five minutes of reading or revision each day.

A routine that continues after Ramadan protects what your child learned during the month.
